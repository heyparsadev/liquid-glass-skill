# verify:design-platform#2

_Phase: Verify — independent re-check of findings from [design-platform](design-platform.md)_

## Finding 8 — **CONFIRMED**

> Reduce Transparency: opaque material with subtle border, lensing disabled; Increase Contrast: stark border, tints saturate; Reduce Motion: specular tracking off, morphing replaced with cross-fade or hard cut, materialize suppressed (also 01-api-reference.md lines 494-496)

- **Established truth:** Apple only describes these adaptations qualitatively. WWDC25 'Meet Liquid Glass' (219), verbatim: "Reduced Transparency, makes Liquid Glass frostier and obscures more of the content behind it. Increased contrast, makes elements predominantly black or white and highlights them with a contrasting border and Reduced Motion decreases the intensity of some effects and disables any elastic properties for the material. These are available automatically whenever you use the new material." No Apple source I checked supports the skill's specifics: an opaque material with a subtle border, lensing disabled, tints that saturate, specular tracking off, morphing replaced by a cross-fade or hard cut, materialize suppressed. 'Tints saturate' conflicts with Apple's 'predominantly black or white', and 'opaque' overstates 'frostier'. The same problem is in 01-api-reference.md lines 494-496.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/
- **Notes:** The 219 transcript (apple-video) matches the finding word for word. The other quotes are also verified verbatim. WWDC26 Platforms State of the Union (wwdc2026/102, apple-video): "Liquid Glass seamlessly adapts to a variety of accessibility settings users may choose, such as reducing transparency or increasing contrast." The 'iPhone Duo tech talk' is "Raise the bar with iPhone Duo" (tech-talks/111462, apple-video): "Just like horizontal bars, a vertical bar doesn't have a scroll-edge effect by default. However, it does have a background when the reduced transparency accessibility setting is enabled. Make sure your custom view content stays legible regardless." The Adopting Liquid Glass article (apple-doc) only says these settings "can remove or modify certain effects" and that standard components adapt automatically. HIG Materials says the variants' appearance "can differ" under reduce-transparency or increase-contrast settings and does not mention Reduce Motion. The Glass and glassEffect(_:in:) reference pages and WWDC25 sessions 323, 284 and 356 give no further detail. Caveat: I could not test on a device, so the skill's specifics are undocumented and partly contradicted by Apple's description, not measured to be false.

## Finding 9 — **CONFIRMED**

> Environment values listed: accessibilityReduceTransparency, accessibilityReduceMotion, accessibilityDifferentiateWithoutColor, colorSchemeContrast; no Show Borders / Button Shapes handling

- **Established truth:** Declaration, verbatim: "@backDeployed(before: iOS 26.1, macOS 26.1, tvOS 26.1, watchOS 26.1, visionOS 26.1) var accessibilityShowBorders: Bool { get }". Abstract: "Whether the system preference for Show Borders is enabled." Available on iOS 14.0+, iPadOS 14.0+, Mac Catalyst 14.0+, macOS 11.0+, tvOS 14.0+, visionOS 1.0+ and watchOS 7.0+, none beta. The Discussion text is as the finding quotes it, and continues "...with clearly visible edges so they remain distinguishable at any window size". accessibilityShowButtonShapes has the same availability and carries renamed: "accessibilityShowBorders" with deprecatedAt "1000.0", which means 'to be deprecated' (soft-deprecated, no version yet), not a hard deprecation. The skill (skills/liquid-glass) never mentions either name. 05-accessibility.md lines 29-32 list only the four values the finding names.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/accessibilityshowborders
- **Notes:** Sources are the DocC JSON for accessibilityshowborders and accessibilityshowbuttonshapes (apple-doc). Platforms State of the Union, WWDC26 session 102 (apple-video), verbatim: "And now macOS 27 also supports the “show borders” environment value, just like iOS." Xcode 27 Release Notes, Previews > New Features (apple-other), verbatim: "The canvas overrides picker now includes a Color Scheme Contrast group for previewing content with standard or increased contrast." (181612731) and "The canvas overrides picker now includes a Control Borders group for previewing content with control borders shown or hidden." (181850835). The @backDeployed attribute implies the new name needs the 26.1+ SDK to compile but runs back to iOS 14. A grep of skills/liquid-glass found no ShowBorders, ButtonShapes or Show Borders text; the only hits are in docs/research.

## Finding 10 — **CONFIRMED**

> System-managed adaptations table omits Reduce Bright Effects

- **Established truth:** Declaration, verbatim: "var accessibilityReduceHighlightingEffects: Bool { get }". Abstract: "Whether the system preference for Reduce Bright Effects is enabled." Discussion: "If this property's value is true, controls, such as buttons, should be drawn in such a way that minimizes highlighting and flashing of onscreen elements." Available on iOS, iPadOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS, tvOS, visionOS and watchOS 26.4+, not beta. The skill does not mention it. No Apple source says system Liquid Glass changes under this setting. So it belongs with the environment values that govern your own highlight, flash or shimmer effects (gated on 26.4), not in the 'System-managed adaptations' table.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/accessibilityreducehighlightingeffects
- **Notes:** Source is the DocC JSON (apple-doc). Reduce Bright Effects is not mentioned in WWDC25 219, the WWDC26 State of the Union, the Adopting Liquid Glass article, HIG Materials, or the Glass and glassEffect docs. That supports the finding's own caveat that whether system glass speculars respond is undocumented. Because the API is 26.4+, code using it needs availability gating, either a deployment target of 26.4 or later or an @available-gated helper view. A grep of skills/liquid-glass found no 'bright effects' or 'HighlightingEffects'.

## Finding 11 — **PARTIALLY**

> Body text (< 18pt regular, < 14pt bold) 4.5:1; Large text (≥ 18pt regular, ≥ 14pt bold) 3:1 (also 02-hig-principles.md line 142)

- **Established truth:** HIG Accessibility, verbatim: "Accessibility Inspector uses the following values from WCAG Level AA as guidance". The table reads: "Up to 17 pts | All | 4.5:1"; "18 pts | All | 3:1"; "All | Bold | 3:1". The next lines are: "If your app doesn’t provide this minimum contrast by default, ensure it at least provides a higher contrast color scheme when the system setting Increase Contrast is turned on. If your app supports Dark Mode, make sure to check the minimum contrast in both light and dark appearances." APCA is named as an alternative standard. The skill's '< 14pt bold → 4.5:1' differs from Apple's 'All | Bold | 3:1'. Its 18 pt threshold matches Apple's.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility
- **Notes:** Source is the HIG JSON (apple-other). Its change log is latest June 9, 2025. I rated this 'partially' for two reasons. The HIG data is quoted accurately, and it is what Accessibility Inspector uses; the skill itself recommends that tool at line 67, so the skill should match Apple's values. However, the skill's thresholds are WCAG 2.x's literal 'large scale text' definition ('at least 18 point or 14 point bold'), and the skill labels the column 'WCAG AA'. I could confirm that definition only through secondary search results, because w3.org did not resolve from this sandbox. So this is a mismatch with Apple's values, stricter for small bold text, rather than an outright factual error. The 'Non-text UI 3:1' row has no counterpart in the HIG table. 02-hig-principles.md line 142 does not define large text and is not wrong on its own.

## Finding 12 — **REFUTED**

> Apple's minimum tappable area is 44 × 44 points

- **Established truth:** The skill's line matches Apple's HIG. HIG Buttons, Best practices, verbatim: "As a general rule, a button needs a hit region of at least 44x44 pt — in visionOS, 60x60 pt — to ensure that people can select it easily, whether they use a fingertip, a pointer, their eyes, or a remote." The finding's HIG Accessibility table is quoted accurately: iOS/iPadOS default control size 44x44 pt and minimum 28x28 pt; macOS 28x28/20x20; tvOS 66x66/56x56; visionOS 60x60/28x28; watchOS 44x44/28x28; about 12 pt of padding around bezeled elements and about 24 pt around non-bezeled ones. But that table is about control size, not hit region, so it does not make the 44x44 tappable-area statement wrong.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons
- **Notes:** Both pages come from the HIG JSON (apple-other). The Buttons change log is latest December 16, 2025, 'Updated guidance for Liquid Glass'. Making 28x28 the minimum tappable area, as the finding implies, would contradict the Buttons page. An optional improvement that changes nothing: cite 'hit region at least 44x44 pt (60x60 pt in visionOS)' from HIG Buttons, and add the Accessibility table's 28x28 pt minimum control size plus the 12/24 pt spacing guidance. Reading the two pages together as 'a visible control can be smaller while its hit region stays 44x44' is my inference, not Apple's wording.

## Finding 13 — **CONFIRMED**

> "Never glass-on-glass-on-glass. Maximum two layers." (02-hig-principles.md line 65: "A glass card sitting on a glass nav bar = OK"; checklist line 21 'Maximum two glass layers stacked')

- **Established truth:** Apple's guidance is to avoid glass on glass entirely, not to allow up to two layers. WWDC25 'Meet Liquid Glass' (219), verbatim: "Similarly, always avoid glass on glass Stacking Liquid Glass elements on top of each other can quickly make the interface feel cluttered and confusing. When placing elements on top of Liquid Glass, avoid applying the material to both layers. Instead, use fills, transparency, and vibrancy for the top elements to make them feel like a thin overlay that is part of the material." HIG Materials: "Don’t use Liquid Glass in the content layer. ... Instead, use standard materials for elements in the content layer, such as app backgrounds. An exception to this is for controls in the content layer with a transient interactive element like sliders and toggles...". This contradicts SKILL.md:85 ('Maximum two layers'), 02-hig-principles.md:65 ('A glass card sitting on a glass nav bar = OK') and pre-ship-checklist.md:21.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/
- **Notes:** More Apple sources agree. The Adopting Liquid Glass article (apple-doc): "avoid overcrowding or layering Liquid Glass elements on top of each other." WWDC25 284, UIKit (apple-video): "This prevents glass elements from overlapping other glass elements, and keeps the illusion of a single floating layer of glass intact." WWDC25 323, SwiftUI (apple-video): "However, glass can not sample other glass, so having nearby glass elements in different containers will result in inconsistent behavior." Calling a 'glass card' content layer is an inference, but it follows directly from the HIG. Two points to keep: the HIG exception for transient controls such as sliders and toggles, and the fact that system containers handle nested glass themselves (WWDC25 284: "When adding glass to an existing glass container, it adapts its appearance automatically."). Trivial: the transcript has no period after 'glass on glass'.

# verify:swiftui-updates#7

_Phase: Verify — independent re-check of findings from [swiftui-updates](swiftui-updates.md)_

## Finding 39 — **PARTIALLY**

> Skill teaches ToolbarSpacer(.fixed) to add breathing room between glass items as a general technique.

- **Established truth:** The quoted HIG text is real and verbatim. The 'Designing for iPhone Duo' page (change log: September 9, 2026, 'New page...') says under its 'Vertical controls' heading: 'Group related toolbar items instead of spacing them manually. Groups you create with ToolbarItemGroup (SwiftUI) or UIBarButtonItemGroup (UIKit) provide space between items and other groups automatically, and adapt as the available space changes, so avoid adding fixed spacing yourself.' The other quoted bullets also appear verbatim on that page: provide both a title and a symbol, use the system overflow menu, 'In general, don't override the default bar placement', and the standard placement order with Back/Close at the top followed by Done. However, this is iPhone Duo / vertical-axis guidance, not a general ban. Apple's general iOS 26 guidance and samples still teach ToolbarSpacer(.fixed) for splitting items that share a glass background into separate groups. Adopting Liquid Glass: 'You can create a fixed spacer to separate items that share a background using these APIs: SpacerSizing/fixed, ToolbarSpacer'. WWDC25-323: 'I used the new ToolbarSpacer API with fixed spacings to split them into their own group.' The Landmarks toolbar sample uses ToolbarItem, ToolbarSpacer(.fixed), ToolbarItemGroup, ToolbarSpacer(.fixed), ToolbarItem. HIG Toolbars (last updated Dec 16, 2025) says 'insert space between buttons or other items where appropriate'. The skill's example (ToolbarItemGroup + ToolbarSpacer(.fixed) + ToolbarItem) is essentially Apple's own pattern. Recommended fix: describe ToolbarSpacer(.fixed) as splitting items into separate glass groups rather than generic 'breathing room'. Prefer ToolbarItemGroup for related items and use fixed spacers sparingly; Adopting Liquid Glass says to audit 'your use of fixed spacers or custom items, as these can appear inconsistent with system behavior'. Add the iPhone Duo caveat: there, group with ToolbarItemGroup and add no manual fixed spacing.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo
- **Notes:** Sources fetched: HIG iPhone Duo JSON (apple-other); HIG Toolbars JSON (apple-other; its change log has no 2026 entry, so the 'avoid fixed spacing' rule appears only on the Duo page); technologyoverviews/adopting-liquid-glass JSON (apple-doc); swiftui/landmarks-refining-the-system-provided-glass-effect-in-toolbars JSON (apple-doc, sample with ToolbarSpacer(.fixed)); WWDC25 session 323 page (apple-video). ToolbarSpacer: 'nonisolated struct ToolbarSpacer', init(_:placement:), available on iOS, iPadOS, Mac Catalyst and macOS 26.0 only. Its abstract: 'A space item creates visual breaks in the toolbar between items. Spacers can have a standard fixed size or be flexible and push items apart.' SpacerSizing.fixed: 'The spacer will use a pre-defined size determined by the system and the context in which the spacer is used.' The quoted Duo text sits under the 'Vertical controls' heading of the iPhone Duo page.

## Finding 40 — **CONFIRMED**

> Golden rule 5: 'Never use .ultraThinMaterial or other legacy Material values in iOS 26+. They look out of place.' (also checklists/pre-ship-checklist.md:9)

- **Established truth:** Golden rule 5 (SKILL.md:88) and the checklist item (checklists/pre-ship-checklist.md:9) contradict Apple. HIG Materials says verbatim: 'Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer. ... Instead, use standard materials for elements in the content layer, such as app backgrounds.' Under iOS, iPadOS it adds: 'In addition to Liquid Glass, iOS and iPadOS continue to provide four standard materials — ultra-thin, thin, regular (default), and thick — which you can use in the content layer to help create visual distinction.' SwiftUI's Material (iOS 15.0+, macOS 12.0+, watchOS 8.0+, etc.) and ShapeStyle.ultraThinMaterial are not deprecated in the current SDK (deprecated: false on every platform). The other quotes are verbatim: 'Only use clear Liquid Glass for components that appear over visually rich backgrounds.' / 'If the underlying content is bright, consider adding a dark dimming layer of 35% opacity.' / 'The appearance of these variants can differ in response to certain system settings, like if people choose a preferred look for Liquid Glass in their device's settings, or turn on accessibility settings that reduce transparency or increase contrast in the interface.' Correct rule: keep standard Materials for content-layer surfaces and don't swap them for Glass, which is for the controls/navigation layer. Do remove custom material or visual-effect backgrounds from system bars, sheets and popovers. Adopting Liquid Glass: 'Reduce your use of custom backgrounds in controls and navigation elements' and 'Audit the backgrounds of sheets and popovers... remove those custom background views'.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials
- **Notes:** Sources: HIG Materials JSON (apple-other; latest change-log entry 'September 9, 2025: Updated guidance for Liquid Glass', with nothing new for iOS 27); swiftui/material JSON and swiftui/shapestyle/ultrathinmaterial JSON (apple-doc). The ultraThinMaterial declaration is '@export(implementation) static var ultraThinMaterial: Material { get }'. Material topics: ultraThin, thin, regular, thick, ultraThick, bar, plus materialActiveAppearance(_:). The skill's suggested replacement ('Use Glass (.regular, .clear, .identity)') would put glass into the content layer, which the HIG explicitly forbids, so the rule is harmful as well as inaccurate.

## Finding 41 — **CONFIRMED**

> `ToolbarItem { Button(...) { }.sharedBackgroundVisibility(.hidden) }`

- **Established truth:** sharedBackgroundVisibility(_:) is an instance method on ToolbarContent only: 'nonisolated func sharedBackgroundVisibility(_ visibility: Visibility) -> some ToolbarContent'. It is available on iOS 26.0, iPadOS 26.0, Mac Catalyst 26.0 and macOS 26.0 (not tvOS, visionOS or watchOS). Abstract: 'Controls the visibility of the glass background effect on items in the toolbar...'. Discussion: 'Hiding the effect will cause the item to be placed in its own grouping.' No View variant exists: swiftui/view/sharedbackgroundvisibility(_:) returns 404, and the method appears only in ToolbarContent's 'Setting visibility' topics. Apply it to the ToolbarItem, not the Button inside it: ToolbarItem { Button("Profile", systemImage: "person.crop.circle") { } }.sharedBackgroundVisibility(.hidden). The skill's version (lines 347-351) calls it on a Button (a View) and will not compile in Xcode 26 or Xcode 27.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/toolbarcontent/sharedbackgroundvisibility(_:)
- **Notes:** Sources: toolbarcontent/sharedbackgroundvisibility(_:) JSON, view/ path 404, toolbarcontent JSON topics (apple-doc); WWDC25-323 (apple-video). Apple's doc sample reads 'ToolbarItem(placement: principal) { BuildStatus() }.sharedBackgroundVisibility(.hidden)'. The doc text omits the leading dot, and the finding's '.principal' is the correct Swift. WWDC25 sample: 'ToolbarItem { ProfileButton() }.sharedBackgroundVisibility(.hidden)'; transcript: 'Apply the sharedBackgroundVisibility modifier to separate an item into its own group without a background.' Xcode 27's ContentBuilder is just 'typealias ContentBuilder = ViewBuilder' and adds no View member, so the verdict holds for iOS 27.

## Finding 42 — **CONFIRMED**

> `ToolbarItem(placement: .bottomBar) { DefaultToolbarItem(kind: .search, placement: .bottomBar) }` (also patterns/glass-search-field.md:59-60)

- **Established truth:** DefaultToolbarItem is 'nonisolated struct DefaultToolbarItem' and conforms only to ToolbarContent, not View. Its initializer is 'nonisolated init(kind: ToolbarDefaultItemKind, placement: ToolbarItemPlacement = .automatic)'. Both the struct and the initializer are available on iOS, iPadOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS, tvOS, visionOS and watchOS 26.0. ToolbarItem is 'nonisolated struct ToolbarItem<ID, Content> where Content : View', so a DefaultToolbarItem cannot be ToolbarItem content. Place it directly in the toolbar: .toolbar { DefaultToolbarItem(kind: .search, placement: .bottomBar) }. ToolbarDefaultItemKind (the type is iOS 17.0+) has exactly three members: sidebarToggle, search and title. The .search member is available on iOS, iPadOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS and visionOS 26.0: 'The search default item kind can be placed in the bottomBar on all supported platforms. On iOS, it can also be placed in the top bar.'
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/defaulttoolbaritem/init(kind:placement:)
- **Notes:** Sources: defaulttoolbaritem, defaulttoolbaritem/init(kind:placement:), toolbaritem, toolbaritem/init(placement:content:), toolbardefaultitemkind and toolbardefaultitemkind/search JSON (apple-doc); WWDC25-323 (apple-video). Apple's init sample and the WWDC25 'InboxView' sample both put DefaultToolbarItem(kind: .search, placement: .bottomBar) directly among ToolbarItems and ToolbarSpacers. In the Xcode 27 SDK, ToolbarItem.init(placement:content:) takes '@ContentBuilder content: () -> Content', but the struct-level 'Content : View' constraint remains, so the code is still a compile error. Both affected spots are wrong: references/01-api-reference.md:419-425 and patterns/glass-search-field.md:58-62. Minor nit: the finding's declaration omits the '= .automatic' default on placement.

## Finding 43 — **CONFIRMED**

> tabBarMinimizeBehavior lists .automatic, .onScrollDown and .never only.

- **Established truth:** TabBarMinimizeBehavior ('struct TabBarMinimizeBehavior'; iOS, iPadOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS, tvOS, visionOS and watchOS 26.0) has exactly four type properties. automatic: 'Determine the behavior automatically based on the surrounding context.' never: 'Never minimize the tab bar.' onScrollDown: 'Minimize the tab bar when downwards scrolling starts. Minimizing is supported for tab bars on only iPhone.' onScrollUp: 'Minimize the tab bar when upwards scrolling starts. Minimizing is supported for tab bars on only iPhone.' The skill (references/01-api-reference.md:372-377) omits .onScrollUp. At member level, onScrollDown and onScrollUp are available on iOS, iPadOS and Mac Catalyst 26.0 only. The modifier is 'nonisolated func tabBarMinimizeBehavior(_ behavior: TabBarMinimizeBehavior) -> some View' (all 7 platforms, 26.0).
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/tabbarminimizebehavior
- **Notes:** Sources: tabbarminimizebehavior, /onscrollup, /onscrolldown and view/tabbarminimizebehavior(_:) JSON (apple-doc); Adopting Liquid Glass (apple-doc): 'configure the tab bar to minimize when a person scrolls down or up. The tab bar expands when a person scrolls in the opposite direction.' The skill's error is an omission rather than a false statement. Separately, the skill labels .automatic '(default)', but neither the modifier page nor the type page states a default, so that comment is unverified. Member pages for automatic and never were not fetched individually.

## Finding 44 — **CONFIRMED**

> `ScrollView(.horizontal) { … }.scrollExtensionMode(.underSidebar)`

- **Established truth:** SwiftUI has no scrollExtensionMode(_:) and no .underSidebar. swiftui/view/scrollextensionmode(_:) returns 404. The name is also absent from the Scroll views topic list, the SwiftUI updates page (June 2025, June 2026 and September 2026 sections), WWDC25-323, Adopting Liquid Glass and the Landmarks sample. The replacement APIs the finding lists are real and match its signatures and availability:
- 'nonisolated func scrollEdgeEffectStyle(_ style: ScrollEdgeEffectStyle?, for edges: Edge.Set) -> some View': iOS, iPadOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS, tvOS and watchOS 26.0 (no visionOS).
- ScrollEdgeEffectStyle: automatic, hard and soft (static vars); the type is on all 7 platforms, 26.0.
- 'scrollEdgeEffectHidden(_ hidden: Bool = true, for edges: Edge.Set = .all)': same six platforms, 26.0.
- safeAreaBar(edge:alignment:spacing:content:): HorizontalEdge and VerticalEdge overloads, all 7 platforms, 26.0.
- backgroundExtensionEffect() and backgroundExtensionEffect(isEnabled:): all 7 platforms, 26.0.
What the skill's snippet tried to do (a horizontal ScrollView scrolling under the sidebar) needs no modifier. Apple's Landmarks sample: 'the landmark views can scroll underneath the sidebar or inspector. To achieve this effect, the app aligns the scroll views next to the leading and trailing edges of the containing view.' Use backgroundExtensionEffect() for non-scrolling hero imagery.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/scroll-views
- **Notes:** Sources: view/scrollextensionmode(_:) (404), scroll-views, view/scrolledgeeffectstyle(_:for:), scrolledgeeffectstyle, view/scrolledgeeffecthidden(_:for:), view/safeareabar(edge:alignment:spacing:content:), view/backgroundextensioneffect() and view/backgroundextensioneffect(isenabled:) JSON (apple-doc); updates/swiftui JSON (apple-doc); landmarks-building-an-app-with-liquid-glass JSON (apple-doc); WWDC25-323 (apple-video). Caveat: scrollEdgeEffect* and safeAreaBar deal with legibility under bars, so they are not a direct substitute for 'scroll under sidebar'. The Landmarks edge-alignment guidance is the accurate replacement. backgroundExtensionEffect is a separate View modifier and is not in the Scroll views topic list; the finding does not claim it is.

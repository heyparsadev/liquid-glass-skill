## Finding 16 — **CONFIRMED**

> Anti-pattern #5 "Right" fix: `view.glassEffect(.regular, isEnabled: visible)`. Also 02 L133 and L166 ("use .glassEffect(... isEnabled: false)" / "animate isEnabled"), 06 L63 ("isEnabled: false") and 06 L162 (`.glassEffect(.regular, isEnabled: !isIdle)`).

- **Established truth:** glassEffect has one documented overload, and it has no isEnabled parameter: `nonisolated func glassEffect(_ glass: Glass = .regular, in shape: some Shape = DefaultGlassEffectShape()) -> some View`. It is available on iOS, iPadOS, Mac Catalyst, macOS, tvOS and watchOS 26.0 (beta:false, deprecated:false). visionOS is not listed. The doc paths swiftui/view/glasseffect(_:in:isenabled:).json and glasseffect(_:isenabled:).json both return HTTP 404. The SwiftUI updates page has no glass API in its June 2026 or September 2026 sections. Apple documents a different way to add or remove glass with a system animation. You add or remove the view that carries glassEffect inside a GlassEffectContainer, using glassEffectTransition(_:) with .matchedGeometry (the default within the container's spacing) or .materialize, plus withAnimation. From 'Applying Liquid Glass to custom views': "If you prefer to have a simpler transition or to create a custom transition, use the materialize transition and withAnimation(_:_:) ... The system applies more than opacity changes with the available transition types." Glass.identity is documented as `static var identity: Glass`: "The identity variant of glass. When applied, your content remains unaffected as if no glass effect was applied." No Apple page says whether switching to .identity animates.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/glasseffect(_:in:)
- **Notes:** Sources: Apple doc JSON for glasseffect(_:in:), glass, glass/identity, glasseffecttransition, view/glasseffecttransition(_:) and applying-liquid-glass-to-custom-views (all apple-doc). I confirmed every cited skill line: 07 L127, 02 L133, 02 L166, 06 L63, 06 L162. Background: an isEnabled overload did exist in the June 2025 beta. Apple Developer Forums thread 787661 (June 2025, visionOS 26 beta 1) quotes the compiler error "'glassEffect(_:in:isEnabled:)' is unavailable in visionOS". The skill probably copied that pre-release signature, and some third-party references still repeat it (secondary). The same stale beta form also appears in 01-api-reference L7, L19, L29 and L51. A related error is at 01 L224/L231, which shows `glassEffectTransition(_:isEnabled:)`. The current declaration is `@MainActor @preconcurrency func glassEffectTransition(_ transition: GlassEffectTransition) -> some View`. The finding's proposed fix is accurate.

## Finding 19 — **CONFIRMED**

> "Specular highlights | Device gyroscope | Automatic — no code". Also SKILL L97 ("picks up specular highlights from device motion") and 02 L39 ("Reacts to device motion (gyroscope-driven)").

- **Established truth:** WWDC25 219 'Meet Liquid Glass' says: "Light sources inside of this environment shine on the material producing highlights that respond to geometry just as you'd expect. On interactions, such as locking and unlocking your phone, these lights move in space, causing light to travel around the material, defining its silhouette. And in some cases, the lighting responds to device motion..." Apple never says 'gyroscope', and never says highlights on app glass generally follow device motion. On Reduce Motion, Apple says only: "Reduced Motion decreases the intensity of some effects and disables any elastic properties for the material." It does not say Reduce Motion turns off 'specular tracking'. For iOS 27, the WWDC26 Platforms State of the Union (session 102) says: "And to establish more depth and separation, we also introduced a darkened edge along with brighter specular highlights." It adds that apps get these improvements "automatically ... without even needing to recompile."
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/
- **Notes:** Both WWDC quotes match the transcripts word for word (apple-video). The words 'specular' and 'gyroscope' do not appear in the 219 transcript. HIG Motion, HIG Accessibility and HIG Materials do not mention specular highlights, device motion or a gyroscope. The finding leaves out one Apple statement. The Apple Newsroom release of June 9, 2025 says: "Liquid Glass uses real-time rendering and dynamically reacts to movement with specular highlights." I saw this sentence only in a search-result snippet from apple.com; apple.com does not resolve from this environment, so I could not open the page. 'Movement' there is ambiguous, but it does loosely support SKILL L97. The fix should keep Apple's hedged wording ('in some cases, the lighting responds to device motion'). It should not delete the idea, and it should not say 'gyroscope-driven'. The 04 L11 table row is the most overstated line. 05-accessibility L15 and 04 L214 ('Specular tracking off' / 'Disables specular highlight tracking') are also unsupported.

## Finding 20 — **PARTIALLY**

> "Adaptive shadows | Soft shadow underneath, tinted by the content behind"

- **Established truth:** The adaptive-shadow behavior Apple describes is about opacity: "The element is aware of what's behind it and increases the opacity of its shadow when it is over text. Conversely, it lowers the opacity of its shadow when it is over a solid light background." ... "As text scrolls underneath, shadows become more prominent to create additional separation." The finding's 'not tint' goes too far, though. For larger elements, WWDC25 219 also says: "On larger elements, like sidebars, the appearance of Liquid Glass is informed by the ambient environment within the app. Light from colorful content nearby can subtly spill onto its surface ... And the effect isn't limited to the surface, the light reflects, scatters, and bleeds into the shadow as well – much like it would in the physical world." Larger or morphed glass also "casts deeper, richer shadows". The skill's 'tinted by the content behind' is still wrong as a general description. A correct row would say: shadow opacity adapts to what is behind; on large elements like sidebars, colored light from nearby content can bleed into the shadow.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/
- **Notes:** All quotes are taken word for word from the WWDC25 219 transcript (apple-video). The finding's opacity quote is accurate. It is marked partially only because the finding did not mention the light-bleeds-into-shadow passage, which is about color and contradicts a flat 'not tint'. The skill line, 02 L40, is confirmed.

## Finding 21 — **CONFIRMED**

> "Glass over a flat color = blob." Also 02 L67 ("looks like a gray blob"), 03 L96 ("Solid bright color ❌ avoid — kills lensing") and 07 L66/L71 ("just flat color again").

- **Established truth:** No Apple source says glass over a flat or solid color looks like a 'blob' or 'kills lensing'. WWDC25 219 says the opposite about Regular: "Regular is the most versatile ... It works in any size, over any content and anything can be placed on top of it." It also says "whereas the Regular variant can work anywhere". Shadows lower their opacity "over a solid light background". Apple's own HIG artwork shows "several Liquid Glass components ... atop a neutral background. The transparent components cast shadows on the background". Apple gives two real constraints. First, layering: HIG Materials says "Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer" and to use standard materials for app backgrounds. Second, the Clear variant: "Clear should only be used when these 3 conditions are met. First, the element you're applying it to is over media-rich content..." HIG Materials adds: "Only use clear Liquid Glass for components that appear over visually rich backgrounds", with a 35% dark dimming layer over bright content. Testing legibility over colorful scrolling content is still good advice. HIG Color: "make sure its default or resting state — like the top of a screen of scrollable content — maintains clear legibility."
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/
- **Notes:** Sources: the WWDC25 219 transcript (apple-video), and the HIG Materials, Color and Accessibility JSON (apple-other). Every quote in the finding is accurate. Clarification for the fix: the skill's 'needs varied/rich content' rule is correct for the Clear variant only, and wrong as a general rule. The skill reaches the right answer for anti-pattern #3 in 07 (don't glass the whole screen background), but for the wrong reason: Apple's reason is the content-layer rule, not a 'blob'. HIG Color also treats glass over monochromatic backgrounds as normal ('in apps with primarily monochromatic content or backgrounds, choosing your brand color as the app accent color can be an effective way...'). I confirmed all cited skill lines: SKILL L91, 02 L67, 03 L96, 07 L66 and L71.

## Finding 22 — **CONFIRMED**

> "Apple's Human Interface Guidelines for iOS 26 are organized around three pillars: Hierarchy, Harmony, and Consistency", with quoted text at L10 ("Establish a clear visual hierarchy where controls and interface elements elevate and distinguish the content beneath them."), L17 (Harmony, formatted as a quote) and L24 ("Adopt platform conventions so the design adapts across window sizes and displays.").

- **Established truth:** The HIG is not organized around Hierarchy, Harmony and Consistency. The trio comes from two places. One is the Liquid Glass technology overview: "embrace the design principles of Apple platforms to create beautiful interfaces that establish hierarchy, create harmony, and maintain consistency across devices and platforms." The other is the WWDC25 Platforms State of the Union, as "three guiding principles for implementing the new design in your apps ... First, establishing hierarchy. Second, creating harmony. And third, maintaining consistency." The HIG landing page's topics are Getting started, Foundations, Patterns, Components, Inputs and Technologies. Its 'Design principles' page (change log, June 8, 2026: "Reintroduced design principles.") lists eight principles: Purpose, Agency, Responsibility, Familiarity, Flexibility, Simplicity, Craft, Delight. They are covered in WWDC26 session 250 'Principles of great design', which never mentions 'harmony' or Liquid Glass.
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles
- **Notes:** Sources: HIG JSON for design-principles and the landing page, the design 'What's new' page (apple-other), the technologyoverviews/liquid-glass and adopting-liquid-glass JSON (apple-doc), and the WWDC25 102, WWDC25 356 and WWDC26 250 transcripts (apple-video). I searched for the L10 and L24 sentences and could not find them on any of these pages, or on developer.apple.com/design. One secondary site (createwithswift, which the skill itself cites) attributes L10 to 'HIG (iOS 26)'. I could not confirm that on any Apple page. L17 is uncited text in a blockquote, and a web search found it nowhere. The finding should add the WWDC25 State of the Union as a second origin of the trio. Separately, 02's Sources section labels 'Get to know the new design system' as WWDC25 session 284. Session 284 is 'Build a UIKit app with the new design'; 'Get to know the new design system' is session 356.

## Finding 23 — **CONFIRMED**

> Presented as a quotation: "Glass exists to make content shine, not to be the show."

- **Established truth:** "Glass exists to make content shine, not to be the show" is not an Apple quote. Exact-phrase web searches found it nowhere, and it is not in HIG Materials, the Liquid Glass overview, Adopting Liquid Glass, or the WWDC25 102, 219 and 356 transcripts. Apple's actual wording is as follows. HIG Materials: "Liquid Glass seeks to bring attention to the underlying content, and overusing this material in multiple custom controls can provide a subpar user experience by distracting from that content." Liquid Glass overview: "Be judicious with your use of color in controls and navigation so they stay legible and allow your content to infuse them and shine through."
- **Evidence:** https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials
- **Notes:** Both of the finding's Apple quotes match the HIG Materials JSON and the technologyoverviews/liquid-glass JSON word for word. Other Apple lines that could replace the made-up quote: the WWDC25 Platforms State of the Union says "And on watchOS, Liquid Glass controls allow content to shine through." Adopting Liquid Glass says "Style updates to list-based layouts help you organize and showcase your content so it can shine through the Liquid Glass layer." WWDC25 219 says Liquid Glass "is best reserved for the navigation layer that floats above the content of your app." I confirmed the skill line, 02 L121.
